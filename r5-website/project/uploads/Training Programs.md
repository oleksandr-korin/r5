# 04 — Training & Programs

Standalone design brief for the **in-gym programs page**. Paste into
Claude Design with the R5 design-system output. Desktop (~1280) +
mobile (~390).

> Uses the **R5 brand design system**. Bilingual UA/EN. Apply the system;
> don't re-specify colour/type.

---

## Purpose & goal

Tell a local prospect exactly **how they can train at R5** and **how to
start**, with zero ambiguity. Clarity converts: someone deciding between
gyms should immediately see the options, understand the beginner path,
and book. This page handles the **in-gym** offerings; remote coaching has
its own page (`05`) and is only teased here.

**Primary CTA:** *Book a free first session.*
**Secondary CTA (one link, low emphasis):** *Prefer to train online? →*

---

## Desktop structure (top → bottom)

1. **Header.** Heavy headline (*Train at R5* / *Ways to train*), one line
   framing the philosophy — coached, scalable, for every level. Cream
   canvas; can open with a slim photo band.

2. **The programs — card set.** The core of the page. A row/grid of the
   in-gym offerings, each a textured R5 card with heavy title, one-line
   description, who it's for, and a CTA. Typical set (confirm with
   Dmytro):
   - **Group classes** — the daily WOD, coached, community-driven.
   - **Personal training** — 1-to-1 with Dmytro or coaches.
   - **Beginner on-ramp / foundations** — the "never done CrossFit"
     entry path. *Lead with this for nervous first-timers.*
   - **Open gym** (if offered).
   Each card: title, 1–2 lines, "good for…", price-or-"see pricing" hook,
   CTA.

3. **New here? The on-ramp path.** A reassurance block specifically for
   beginners: you don't need to be fit to start; everything scales; first
   session is free and coached. This is the anxiety-killer; give it
   space. "Manual page" panel works.

4. **How a session runs.** Simple step strip — warm-up → strength → WOD →
   cool-down — so the unfamiliar becomes concrete. Marker/chalk numbering
   fits.

5. **The R5 method note (HRV).** A short band tying training to the
   recovery-science approach — "we train hard *and* program your recovery"
   — linking to the Coach page for depth. Differentiator reminder.

6. **Pricing / membership.** Clear table or card set of options
   (drop-in / monthly / personal-training rates). Honest and legible. If
   prices aren't public yet, a "request pricing" CTA instead.

7. **Online teaser (low emphasis).** One slim line + link for the remote
   audience → Online Coaching page. Must not compete with local CTAs.

8. **Persistent CTA band.** *Book a free first session.*

9. **Footer.** Global.

---

## Mobile adaptation (~390)

- **Programs cards (2):** full-width stacked cards, on-ramp card pulled to
  the **top** of the stack (beginners are the most hesitant, most common
  first-timer).
- **Session steps (4):** vertical numbered stack.
- **Pricing (6):** stacked option cards rather than a wide table; one
  clear recommended/most-popular option visually flagged.
- **Sticky CTA** appears on scroll.
- Touch targets 44px+, CTAs 56px.

---

## Content blocks needed (placeholders)

- Program list + descriptions + who-each-is-for (confirm exact offerings
  with Dmytro).
- On-ramp / beginner copy (UA/EN).
- Session-structure steps.
- Pricing — *confirm with Dmytro; if not public, use "request pricing."*
- HRV-method one-liner.

---

## Bilingual notes

- Program names and "good for…" lines must read naturally in both
  languages — avoid CrossFit jargon that doesn't translate; keep plain.
- Pricing tables: ensure currency/format and labels are clear in UA and
  EN; Ukrainian labels run longer — leave room.

## What to design (paste into Claude Design)

Design the **Training & Programs page** for R5 (in-gym offerings),
desktop (~1280) and mobile (~390), in the R5 brand system. Goal: make it
obvious how to train here and how to start, and convert to a free first
session. Sections: heavy header framing the philosophy → a textured card
set of programs (group classes, personal training, beginner on-ramp, open
gym) each with title / one-liner / who-it's-for / CTA → a beginner
"new here?" reassurance panel → a "how a session runs" numbered step strip
(marker/chalk) → a short HRV-method band linking to the coach → clear
pricing/membership cards → a low-emphasis online-coaching teaser link →
persistent "book a free session" CTA → footer. Whiteboard energy, heavy
Cyrillic-capable display type, honest and legible. Show Ukrainian and
English. Mobile: programs stack full-width with the beginner on-ramp card
on top, session steps vertical, pricing as stacked cards with one flagged
recommended option, sticky CTA on scroll.
